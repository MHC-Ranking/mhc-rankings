The Colley Matrix Method

The Colley Matrix is a bias-free, mathematically robust ranking system originally designed for college football. It uses a system of linear equations to distribute ratings such that every team's rating depends on the ratings of the teams they played.

The fundamental equation solved is:

$$ C \vec{r} = \vec{b} $$

Where:

  • \( C \) is the Colley coefficient matrix. The diagonal elements \( C_{i,i} \) equal \( 2 + N_i \) (where \( N_i \) is the total number of games team \( i \) played). The off-diagonal elements \( C_{i,j} \) equal the negative number of times team \( i \) played team \( j \).
  • \( \vec{r} \) is the vector of ratings we are solving for.
  • \( \vec{b} \) is the vector where each element \( b_i = 1 + \frac{W_i - L_i}{2} \) (where \( W_i \) and \( L_i \) are the wins and losses of team \( i \)).

Strength of Schedule (SOS) is subsequently calculated as the average rating of a team's opponents.
